Onboarding Kit Checklist: A Guide for HR Teams

What to include, what to skip, and how to plan it before the next hiring wave.

← Back to Blog

Most HR teams plan onboarding kits reactively: a new hire's start date is a week away and someone scrambles to put a box together. A checklist you can reuse every hiring cycle turns this into a five-minute task instead of a weekly fire drill. Here's the version we hand our own HR clients.

Before You Order

  • Confirm headcount and start dates. Batch onboarding (a hiring wave) is cheaper per unit than ordering kits one at a time.
  • Lock the logo file. Vector format (AI, EPS, SVG). A low-res PNG pulled from a slide deck will look rough on print.
  • Decide the budget tier. Not every hire needs the same kit: a standard kit for most roles, a premium one for senior hires, is a reasonable split.

What Actually Belongs in the Box

  • One daily-use item: a notebook, mug, or water bottle. This is the item that gets used, not shelved.
  • Apparel, if your culture supports it: a t-shirt or hoodie people would wear outside the office, not just at a company event.
  • A tech essential: a power bank or wireless charger reads as genuinely useful, not filler.
  • A welcome note: costs nothing, changes how the whole kit feels.
  • ID/access essentials if applicable: lanyard, badge holder.

What to Skip

Stress balls, generic keychains, and anything that reads as "leftover conference swag" gets associated with the brand exactly as much as a branded pen does, which is to say, barely at all. If a budget line exists for a filler item, it's usually better spent upgrading the one item every hire actually uses.

Timeline to Plan Around

For a standard kit, allow 10-14 working days from confirmed brief to delivery, longer around Eid and year-end when production queues fill up. If onboarding happens continuously, a standing order arrangement removes the need to re-brief a vendor every single hire.

Budgeting It

A reasonable starting point: treat onboarding kits as a fixed per-hire cost in the HR budget, not a discretionary spend decided after the offer is signed. This makes the kit consistent regardless of who's doing the hiring that quarter.
