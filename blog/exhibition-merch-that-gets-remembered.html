How to Choose Exhibition Merch That Gets Remembered

Most exhibition giveaways end up in a bin. Here's how to pick merch people keep and show off.

← Back to Blog

Walk any exhibition hall floor an hour after closing and you'll see the evidence: pens, stress balls, and flimsy tote bags abandoned in bins across the venue. Exhibition merch has a bad reputation for good reason: most of it is chosen for unit cost, not for what happens after the visitor walks away. Here's how to break that pattern.

1. Solve a Problem, Don't Just Brand an Object

The best exhibition merch answers "why would I carry this around for the rest of the day?" A portable charger for a long conference day, a decent tote to carry other swag in, or a reusable water bottle at a warm-weather event all solve an immediate problem, which is why people keep them.

2. Match the Item to Your Booth Traffic

High-traffic booths need something cheap enough to give away in volume without straining budget, but "cheap" should mean simple, not poorly made. A basic item that works properly beats a fancier one that breaks in someone's bag before they leave the venue.

3. Design for the Second Life

The real value of exhibition merch isn't the moment of handoff. It's every time the item gets used afterward, at a desk, in a bag, at home. A branded notebook that gets used in meetings for months keeps your name in front of the recipient long after the event ends, at zero extra cost to you.

4. Tier Your Giveaways

Not every booth visitor needs the same item. A light giveaway for general foot traffic, and a more premium item reserved for qualified leads who actually stop for a conversation, makes the better items feel earned, and gives your booth staff a natural reason to engage visitors before handing anything over.

5. Order Early, Not the Week Before

Exhibition dates don't move, and branded merchandise needs real production time: sourcing, branding, quality checks, and packing all take longer than people expect. Locking in your merch order 4-6 weeks before the event avoids the scramble (and the compromise on quality) that comes with last-minute ordering.
